## The 18 rules (complete)

- **1. Participation** — solo or a team of up to 4 members; each participant may only be part of one team.
- **2. Required technology** — FalkorDB must be the primary graph database in the project, and it must support a central part of what the product does.
- **3. The graph has to do real work** — a visualisation drawn on top of ordinary storage does not qualify: **if the product still works once FalkorDB is taken out, the graph is decoration rather than the thing the product runs on.**
- **4. How to run FalkorDB** — Docker, a self-hosted instance, and FalkorDB Cloud are all accepted; any language, framework, frontend, or deployment platform may be used alongside it.
- **5. Tracks** — open-ended within the three tracks (Agents That Act on Connected Data, Agent Memory and Coordination, Company Brain). You may enter any or all of them, and **you must name every track you are entering when you submit**.
- **6. Data** — use data you are allowed to use: public, synthetic, or your own. **Do not load private, login-protected, paywalled, personal, or otherwise restricted information into your graph.**
- **7. Timing** — the hackathon runs from October 15, 12:01 AM IST to October 18, 11:59 PM IST. Submissions close at the end of that window.
- **8. New work** — the main coding and design work must begin after the hackathon starts. **You may discuss ideas, create notes, sketch the graph model, or prepare diagrams beforehand.**
- **9. Reuse** — frameworks, open-source libraries, public APIs, templates, third-party tools, and publicly available datasets and assets are allowed. **The original work completed during the hackathon will be judged.**
- **10. Every submission must include:**
  - A public source-code repository
  - A README with setup instructions and your graph data model
  - The Cypher queries or graph algorithms the product depends on
  - A demo video showing the working project
  - A live deployment, or complete local setup instructions if it cannot be deployed
  - The track you are entering
- **11. AI disclosure** — AI coding assistants are allowed, but **their use must be disclosed**.
- **12. Understanding** — participants must understand the submitted code and be able to explain the graph model, the queries, the project architecture, and the technical decisions behind them.
- **13. AI-generated work** — projects that are entirely generated using AI without meaningful participant contribution, verification, or technical understanding may be rejected.
- **14. Prizes** — prize pool worth $20,000; best project in each track wins: **iPhone 18 Pro** (Agents That Act on Connected Data), **PS5 with GTA VI** (Agent Memory and Coordination), **M6 Mac Mini** (Company Brain). Every member of a winning team gets the prize.
- **15. IP** — any intellectual property developed during the hackathon belongs to the participant or team that created it; teams are encouraged to agree internally on ownership before submitting.
- **16. Conduct** — treat participants, organisers, sponsors, speakers, judges, and community members with respect.
- **17. Disqualification** — harassment, discrimination, plagiarism, or attempts to manipulate the judging process will result in disqualification.
- **18. Compliance** — failure to follow these rules or the WeMakeDevs Code of Conduct may result in disqualification.

## Hackathon relevance

- The **removal test** (rule 3) is the single sharpest filter to apply to any project idea: since the guide and notes recommend building with FalkorDB as the runtime context layer, this rule rewards that design immediately.
- Rule 8 protects this weekend's preparation work you may already have: notes, graph-model sketches, and diagrams are explicitly allowed beforehand — the *code* must be new.
- Rule 10 is the submission checklist; the automated notes cover every item (graph model guidance, Cypher references, algorithm docs, demo ideas).
- Rule 11 is a compliance step people forget: **disclose your AI coding assistants** in the README or submission.
- Rule 6 constrains data sourcing for the demo — use public/synthetic/own datasets only.
